# Sources for chapter 15, "Who pays for it?"

Written 2026-11-24. The chapter was written by Claude, made by Anthropic, and is partly about Anthropic.

After this file was written, the coordinator reworded the chapter's sentences marked "wording stronger than source" or "not found" here to match their sources (for example "similar quality" for CAISI, "use" for Menlo, attributing the half-plan limit to SemiAnalysis). A row's chapter sentence may therefore differ slightly from the final page; the evidence column is current.

Split from a longer draft on 2026-11-24 (the second half, about companies telling workers to use more AI and then less, became chapter 16), then checked by blind readers and a fairness reader. The rows below were rewritten against the chapter's current prose.

Every page below was opened by a researcher on 2026-11-24 unless the row says otherwise. The
researchers' working notes were scratch files and are not kept, so each row carries its own
quotation, URL, page date and status. "Read" means 2026-11-24 for every row.

Status key: **verified** = a primary page (the company's own page, filing or executive post, or a
verbatim copy of it, or a research group's own report) states it; **news report** = only a
journalist's account; **calculation** = arithmetic on sourced inputs; **analyst estimate**;
**allegation** = court complaint; **author's reasoning** = the chapter's inference or explanation,
which no source states; **not found** = the research does not support it; **wording stronger than
source** = the source supports a weaker statement, and the row says how.

Only sentences of the chapter's prose are listed. Footnotes are checked in the last section but one.

## Disputed or uncertain

Copied from the research files' sections of that name.

- **"Subsidized" is an inference, not an established fact.** The strongest evidence is SemiAnalysis's measured API-equivalent values and its margin arithmetic. But SemiAnalysis's own podcast summary says Anthropic books 50 to 60% gross margins on these plans and that "the catch isn't subsidy". Both can be true: the average subscriber is profitable; a subscriber who uses most of the allowance is not. Neither company publishes a subscription-only margin.
- **The ratios depend on the month, the model and the workload.** For a maxed-out $200 plan: Anthropic about 40x (June 2026) and about 59 to 63x on Opus and Sonnet (October 2026) but about 12x on Fable; OpenAI about 70x (June 2026) falling to about 10 to 15x after the September 2026 cut (which OpenAI then cancelled on 2026-10-29). Few subscribers max out: Anthropic said fewer than 5% would hit the 2025 weekly limits.
- **API-equivalent value overstates cost by the API markup.** SemiAnalysis assumed 75% (June) and 92% (October) API gross margins. At 92%, $12,000 of API-priced tokens costs roughly $1,000 to serve, about 5x a $200 subscription rather than 60x. That is the researcher's arithmetic from SemiAnalysis's assumption, not a measured cost.
- **Anthropic's gross margin swung from about -95% (2024) to about +40% (2025) to about +85% (first half 2026)** on S-1 figures (cost of revenue $752M on $386M; $2,773M on $4,585M; $2,380M on $16,286M). Part of that may be accounting (what counts as cost of revenue; free-tier compute is in sales and marketing), not only cheaper serving.
- **OpenAI 2025 gross margin**: 33% (Tech Times, from leaked accounts) against about 30% (Epoch estimate) against other secondary figures near 43% and a "compute margin" near 70% (SaaStr citing The Information, not opened). "About one-third" is the safest.
- **Ultra 10x vs Max 20x.** The old plan was sold as "20x Pro"; Ultra is "10x Pro usage every week" at the same price. Whether this is a cut depends on whether "20x" referred to the five-hour window or the week; no Anthropic statement comparing the two was found.
- **Third-party ratios of 12:1, 25x and 175:1** in the Tech Times 2026-06-02 article come from community analyses and a YouTuber; not verified.
- **SemiAnalysis says subscriptions are about 10% of Anthropic's revenue**, while the S-1 says 20% for the first half of 2026. The S-1 figure probably includes Team and Enterprise seats; the difference is not explained.
- **OpenAI API standard prices**: the pricing page mixes tiers (standard, batch, flex).
