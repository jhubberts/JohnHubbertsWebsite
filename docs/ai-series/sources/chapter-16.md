# Sources for chapter 16, "Use more, then less"

Written 2026-11-24. The chapter was written by Claude, made by Anthropic, and is partly about Anthropic.

After this file was written, the coordinator reworded the chapter's sentences marked "wording stronger than source" or "not found" here to match their sources (for example "similar quality" for CAISI, "use" for Menlo, attributing the half-plan limit to SemiAnalysis). A row's chapter sentence may therefore differ slightly from the final page; the evidence column is current.

Split from a longer draft on 2026-11-24 (it holds the second half of what was chapter 15), then checked by blind readers and a fairness reader. The rows below were written against the chapter's current prose, reusing the rows of the old chapter 15 where the claim is unchanged.

Every page below was opened by a researcher on 2026-11-24 unless the row says otherwise. The
researchers' working notes were scratch files and are not kept, so each row carries its own
quotation, URL, page date and status. "Read" means 2026-11-24 for every row.

Status key: **verified** = a primary page (the company's own page, filing or executive post, or a
verbatim copy of it, or a research group's own report) states it; **news report** = only a
journalist's account; **calculation** = arithmetic on sourced inputs; **analyst estimate**;
**allegation** = court complaint; **author's reasoning** = the chapter's inference or explanation,
which no source states; **not found** = the research does not support it; **wording stronger than
source** = the source supports a weaker statement, and the row says how.

Only sentences of the chapter's prose are listed. Footnotes are checked in the last section but one.

## Disputed or uncertain

Copied from the research files' sections of that name.

- **Who "shut down" Claudeonomics.** Many outlets say Meta shut it down. Meta told Fortune that the employee took it down "at their discretion" and Meta "did not request this action". The board's own notice cited data being "shared externally".
- **Dollar figures for the Meta leaderboard.** The $1.4 million for the top user is Fortune's own arithmetic (281 billion tokens times $5 per million), not a bill. $900 million for the whole company (Pragmatic Engineer) is an estimate; one aggregator says $9 billion. The chapter uses neither.
- **Meta's token total.** 60 trillion (or 60.2) in 30 days is the April figure; 73.7 trillion appears only on secondary blogs.
- **Who sent the 6,000-person Meta memo.** One secondary site names CTO Andrew Bosworth; Bloomberg Law's excerpt does not. Unconfirmed.
- **Whether "AI Native" labels affected reviews or layoffs.** The complaint alleges it and that a plaintiff was told so (paragraph 95); Meta says the labels were not used in evaluations and that people made the layoff decisions. Key parts are pleaded "on information and belief". No court has found either way.
- **The lawsuit's case number.** Caption 4:26-cv-07122 (Oakland); page header 3:26-cv-07122-WHO. Cite as N.D. Cal. No. 26-cv-07122.
- **Amazon's memo is mixed, not a clean reversal.** It calls usage metrics "deeply flawed" and warns about tokenmaxxing, but also says they "correlate well with the outputs that matter" and keeps using them. Amazon says it has no token caps.
- **Meta's review change.** WIRED called it easing off; Meta called it a clarification of existing practice.
- **Origin of "tokenmaxxing".** In print by 2026-03-20 (NYT). Tunguz's 2026-04-01 post did not claim it. Who first used it is unknown.
- **Uber's budget.** Who said it and when varies between pages.
- **Microsoft's Claude Code cut.** The official reason is "toolchain unification"; cost is the reporters' inference.
- **No large company moving staff to open-weight models.** Found: Meta steering to its own MetaCode, AT&T using less advanced models, small firms (Lindy) moving to DeepSeek, and bottom-up use of open-source models. No big-company memo moving staff onto open-weight models was found.
