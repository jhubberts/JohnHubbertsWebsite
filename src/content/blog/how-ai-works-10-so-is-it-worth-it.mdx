# So is it worth it?

**So is it worth it?**

My mom asked three worried questions, and the answers came with costs and cautions. I meant every one of them. I also use these systems all day, by choice. In this last chapter, I say why. What follows is mine. I wrote it to Claude in my own words, and Claude made it plainer.

## One day, in the background

This whole series was made in one day. I was doing my regular job the whole time. Now and then I looked over, read what Claude had done, and typed a line or two.

## The cost of trying

I could have built every piece of this by hand. I know how to make a website. I know how to copy a model onto my computer and run it. I know how to build something like the drawing box in chapter 2.

It would have taken many hours of tedious work. For a present to one reader, I would never have started.

That is what changed. Trying an idea used to cost me so much time that most ideas were not worth it. Now it costs me almost no time, so I try them. It still costs what chapter 8 counted.

My work has changed the same way. My company has fewer than fifty people. I believe we now do what would have taken a few hundred people a few years ago. That is my own judgment, and not a measurement. Chapter 8 has the other side of it: the people who are not hired.

## What I give up

I spend much of my day talking to AI systems, and less of it in a room with the people I work with. I miss that.

And as I said in chapter 9, I give these systems more trust than I can fully defend.

I also cannot tell you what this one day used in electricity. As chapter 8 said, I could not find a published figure from the company that makes Claude.

## Who else can do this

Learning to write code took me years of school, and it was hard. Someone who never learned to write code could get most of the way to a series like this by asking in plain sentences, as I did. They would get stuck where I knew what to look for: the page that slid under my finger, and the news story the AI had missed.

That someone could be you. A plain chat is the place to start. As chapter 7 said, it can only write text to you.
