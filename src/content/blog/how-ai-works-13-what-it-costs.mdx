# What it costs

**What does all this cost?**

Machines in a building called a <Term id="data-center">data center</Term> work out every answer from a <Term id="chat-system">chat system</Term>. The machines use electricity, and the building uses water to keep them cool.

The companies that run chat systems have started to publish how much electricity and water they use.[^footprint] Their figures are small, they do not agree with each other, and as far as I could find, nobody outside the companies has checked them.[^google-energy] I could not find any published figure from Anthropic, the company that makes the AI that wrote this series.

<OneQuestionsFootprint />

## Small each time, large in total

By the companies' own figures, one text answer uses about as much electricity as an LED light bulb uses in two minutes. That comparison is my own arithmetic: Google and OpenAI give 0.24 and 0.34 <Term id="watt-hour">watt-hours</Term> for one answer, and a typical LED bulb uses 9 watts.[^google-energy][^altman-energy]

All the data centers together use a growing share of the world's electricity. They used about 1.5% of it in 2024.[^iea-2025] The International Energy Agency, an organization of governments that studies energy, expects about 3% by 2030, and it says that AI accounts for almost half of the growth.[^iea-2025][^iea-2026] In the United States, data centers used 4.7% of the electricity in 2024, and Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, a laboratory of the United States government, expects nearly 12% by 2030.[^lbnl-2025]

## What it learned from

The companies say that their <Term id="model">models</Term> learn from text that is publicly available on the internet, text they pay for, and text their users agree to share. They do not publish a list.[^openai-data][^anthropic-data]

Authors and publishers have gone to court over this. In June 2025 a judge in the United States ruled that <Term id="training">training</Term> on books a company had bought was allowed, and that downloading pirated books was not. The company was Anthropic, which makes the AI that wrote this series. It agreed to pay $1.5 billion, about $3,000 a book, and a court approved the payment in July 2026.[^bartz]

In September 2026 an appeals court ruled against a different company, which had copied a rival's work to build a competing product.[^ross] As of late October 2026, a case brought by the New York Times against OpenAI, the company that makes ChatGPT, had not been decided, as far as I could find.

## Jobs

The first studies of AI and jobs do not agree, and they cover only the first few years.

Researchers at Stanford University followed payroll records up to June 2026. They looked at workers aged 22 to 25 in the kinds of job where AI can do the most. About 19% fewer of them were employed than if those jobs had kept pace with other jobs, mostly because fewer were hired. The researchers found no job loss across the economy as a whole. They say that their numbers show a pattern and do not prove that AI caused it.[^stanford]

A study in Denmark found no effect on pay or hours in the first two years after ChatGPT appeared.[^denmark]

## Elections

AI-made fakes did not change the results of the 2024 elections, according to a review by researchers at Columbia University. Before those elections, many people had expected AI-made fakes to change results.[^columbia]

Experiments since then have found that a chat system arguing for a candidate can shift people's answers on a 100-point survey scale by a few points, and by about ten in Canada and Poland.[^persuasion] The people in those experiments were answering a survey. The experiments did not measure votes.

In the United States elections of 2026, the Wesleyan Media Project, a group at Wesleyan University that tracks political ads, counted at least 164 political ads made with AI, costing at least $80 million. Most carried no label saying that AI made them.[^wesleyan] As of November 23, 2026, I had found no study of what effect the ads had.

<JohnsThoughts>All of this scares me profoundly, and is a real problem.</JohnsThoughts>

**So what does it cost?** One answer costs very little. All of it together uses a growing share of the world's electricity. The other costs fall on the people whose writing AI learned from, on people starting their working lives, and on elections. Courts and researchers are still working those costs out.

[^ross]: Nieman Lab, [report on the appeals court ruling for Thomson Reuters against Ross Intelligence](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/09/federal-appeals-court-upholds-thomson-reuters-landmark-ai-copyright-win/), September 30, 2026. This is a news report; I did not read the court order itself.

[^wesleyan]: The Wesleyan Media Project, reported by [CT Mirror](https://ctmirror.org/2026/10/08/wesleyan-project-report-ai-political-advertisements/), October 8, 2026: "Almost 70% of the ads and 78% of the spending did not have a disclaimer."
