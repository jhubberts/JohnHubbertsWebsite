# Who pays for it?

**Who pays for all of this?**

Customers pay part of it, and investors pay the rest. You can pay for a <Term id="chat-system">chat system</Term> in two ways: a flat price each month, like a phone plan, or by the amount of text, like an electric meter. Chapter 14 gave the meter's prices. The two biggest sellers, OpenAI and Anthropic, both spend more than they take in, and the heaviest users of their monthly plans get far more than they pay for.

Claude, the AI made by Anthropic, wrote this chapter, and much of the story is about Anthropic and Claude.

## Heavy users got more than they paid for

Both companies have said that some subscribers used far more than they paid for. Anthropic's plans cost $20, $100 and $200 a month, and OpenAI's plans for ChatGPT, its chat system, cost $20 to $500.[^claude-plans][^chatgpt-plans] In January 2025, Sam Altman, OpenAI's chief executive, wrote that OpenAI was losing money on its $200 plan because "people use it much more than we expected."[^altman] In July 2025, Anthropic said that one person had used "tens of thousands" of dollars' worth of Claude on a $200 plan, and it added weekly limits.[^weekly-limits]

A $200 plan used up to its limit was worth 40 to 70 times its price at the meter, by one firm's estimate. In June 2026, SemiAnalysis, a firm that studies AI companies, put Anthropic's top plan at about 40 times its price and OpenAI's at about 70 times.[^semianalysis-june] Since then, by SemiAnalysis's measure, Anthropic has let its newest model use up only half of a plan's allowance. OpenAI cut its $200 plan in September 2026, then cancelled the cut a month later.[^semianalysis]

## Who covers the cost

The meter price includes the company's profit, so a plan's value at the meter is more than the plan costs the company. SemiAnalysis called the plans "highly subsidized", meaning partly paid for by the company. By its estimate, a subscriber who used a whole plan on one of Anthropic's models would cost Anthropic far more than the $200 they paid, and plans bring in about a tenth of Anthropic's sales but use about 40 in 100 of its computers' time. Two days later, on its podcast, it said that Anthropic still keeps more than half of what the plans bring in, after the cost of serving them.[^semianalysis][^semianalysis-podcast] These estimates are hard to fit together, and the companies do not publish enough to settle them: neither says what a plan costs it to serve.[^s1-compute][^openai-2025]

Both companies spent more than they took in. In 2025, OpenAI lost about $21 billion running its business, on about $13 billion of sales, as journalists reported from its accounts.[^openai-2025] Anthropic lost $8.1 billion on $4.6 billion of sales, by the filing it made to sell its shares on the stock market.[^s1] Investors covered the difference. In 2025, OpenAI raised $40 billion from investors in a single sale of shares, and Anthropic raised $13 billion in another.[^openai-funding][^anthropic-funding]

I found no statement from either company that it prices plans below cost to win customers. SemiAnalysis called the plans tools for winning customers, and Anthropic's filing to the stock market says that subscribers who use up a plan may move to a bigger one or pay at the meter.[^semianalysis][^s1-route]

**So who pays for it?** Customers pay, at the meter or by the month, and investors cover the companies' losses. The heaviest monthly users get far more than they pay for, at the meter's prices, though Anthropic has started to narrow its plans. Chapter 16 tells what happened when large companies paid for their workers' AI.
