# A network that reads handwriting

**What is a neural network, really?**

It is a list of numbers and a set of steps for adding them up. The one on this page reads handwritten digits. It runs on the device you are holding, and your drawing never leaves that device.

Draw a number in the box with your finger.

<DrawADigit />

## What just happened

Nobody told this network what a 7 looks like. It has no rule that says "a 7 has a flat top". It has 25,818 numbers, and they do all the work.

1. Your drawing is shrunk to a grid of 28 by 28 squares. Each square becomes a number, from 0 for empty to 1 for full ink. That makes 784 numbers.
2. Those 784 numbers go to 32 small adding units. Each unit multiplies every number it gets by one of its own, adds up the results along with a starting number of its own, and passes on the total. If the total is below zero, it passes on 0. Then 16 more units do the same with those 32 totals.
3. Ten final units, one for each digit, do the same once more. The biggest total is the answer.

Go back up and tap one of the squares under "Two layers of adding units". You will see that unit's own numbers as a picture: blue where your ink raises its total, orange where your ink lowers it.

## Where the numbers came from

The 25,818 numbers started out random. With random numbers the network gets about one digit in ten right, which is no better than guessing.

Then it practiced. It was shown handwritten digits along with the right answers. After every small group of examples, each of its numbers was nudged a little in whichever direction made its answers less wrong. This was repeated with 60,000 example digits, many times over. No person chose any of the numbers.

After practice it was tested on 10,000 digits it had never seen. It got 9,725 of them right.

### Is this like a brain?

The name comes from the brain, and the comparison is partly right.

**Where it holds.** A unit takes in many signals, counts some more than others, and passes one signal on. Learning changes how strong the connections are. Both are true of real neurons too.

**Where it stops.**

- A real neuron fires all-or-nothing pulses. A unit passes on a number, large or small.
- Real neurons signal across a gap using chemicals. A unit is one line of arithmetic.
- A brain removes connections as it develops, from around birth into a person's twenties. This network's connections never change. Only the numbers on them do.
- A human brain has more than 86 billion neurons. This network has 58 units.

</aside>

**So what is a neural network?** A set of numbers, found by practice on examples, that turns one list of numbers into another.
