# A network that reads handwriting

**What is a neural network, really?**

It is a list of numbers and a set of steps for adding them up. The one on this page reads handwritten digits. It runs on the device you are holding, and your drawing never leaves that device.

Draw a number in the box with your finger.

<DrawADigit />

## What just happened

Nobody told this network what a 7 looks like. It has no rule that says "a 7 has a flat top". It has 25,818 numbers, and they do all the work.

Tap "Show how it got there" under the box and you can follow each step. Your drawing is cut into a grid of 28 by 28 small squares, and each square becomes a number for how much ink is in it. That is 784 numbers. Small adding units combine them, in two rounds, the second working on what the first passed on. Ten final units, one for each digit, each give a total, and the biggest total is the answer.

Each unit does what the machine in chapter 1 did. It multiplies the numbers it gets by numbers of its own, and adds them up. This network is 58 of those, joined together.

## Where the numbers came from

The 25,818 numbers started out random. With random numbers the network gets about one digit in ten right, which is no better than guessing.

Then it practiced. It was shown handwritten digits along with the right answers. After every small group of examples, each of its numbers was nudged a little in whichever direction made its answers less wrong. For each number, arithmetic can tell whether a little more or a little less would have made the answer less wrong. This page skips how. This was repeated with 60,000 example digits, many times over. No person chose any of the numbers.

Move the slider to see the network at six points in its practice.

After practice it was tested on 10,000 digits it had never seen. It got 9,725 of them right.

### Is this like a brain?

The name comes from the brain, and the comparison is partly right.

**Where it holds.** A unit takes in many signals, counts some more than others, and passes one signal on. Learning changes how strong the connections are. Both are true of real neurons too. In a unit's picture, blue marks connections that raise its total and orange marks connections that lower it, much as some synapses excite a nerve cell and others quiet it.

**Where it stops.**

- A real neuron fires all-or-nothing pulses, and signals "how much" by how fast it fires. A unit simply passes on a number.
- Real neurons signal across a gap using chemicals. A unit is one line of arithmetic.
- A brain removes connections as it develops, and forms new ones. This network's connections never change. Only the numbers on them do.
- A human brain has about 86 billion neurons. This network has 58 units.

</aside>

**So what is a neural network?** A set of numbers, found by practice on examples, that turns one list of numbers into another.
