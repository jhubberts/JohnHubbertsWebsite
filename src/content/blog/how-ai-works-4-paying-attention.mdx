# Paying attention

**What changed in 2017?**

What a word means depends on the words around it. Take the word "it" in these two sentences.

_The animal did not cross the street because it was too tired._

_The animal did not cross the street because it was too wide._

In the first, "it" is the animal. In the second, "it" is the street. You worked that out without noticing, from a word that comes later.

To write sensibly, a model has to do the same. In 2017, researchers at Google published a design that lets every word look at every other word in the text, all at once, and take what it needs from them. They called the design the transformer. The looking is called attention.

<WhatDoesItMean />

## What that shows

In one of the model's patterns, "it" switches from the animal to the street when the last word changes. The model has 144 patterns, and most of them do other work. Together they let each word take in what it needs from the rest.

Do not read too much into the picture. It shows where the model looked. It does not show what the model understood.

## Two ways to practise

The transformer was used to build two kinds of model. One practises by filling in a hidden word, with both sides of the gap in view. The other practises by guessing the next word, with only the earlier words in view, as in chapter 3.

<TwoGames />

BERT, which Google introduced in October 2018, plays the first game. The GPT models from OpenAI play the second. A model that can guess the next word can write, and that is the kind chat systems are built on.

## The same design, much bigger

Because the transformer handles all the words at once and not one after another, it could be run on far more text and grown far larger.

- The network in chapter 2 has 25,818 numbers.
- BERT has about 110 million.
- The GPT-2 used in chapter 3 has about 124 million.
- GPT-3, from 2020, has 175 billion.

For the models behind today's chat systems, the companies do not usually publish the number.

**So what changed in 2017?** A design in which every word can draw on every other word at once. It could be grown far larger than anything before it. Today's systems are that design, grown large.
