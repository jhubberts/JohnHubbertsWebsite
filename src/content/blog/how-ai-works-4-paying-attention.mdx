# Paying attention

**What changed in 2017?**

What a word means depends on the words around it. Take the word "it" in these two sentences.

_The animal did not cross the street because it was too tired._

_The animal did not cross the street because it was too wide._

In the first, "it" is the animal. In the second, "it" is the street. You worked that out without noticing, from a word that comes later.

To write sensibly, a model has to do the same. In 2017, researchers at Google published a design that lets every word look at every other word in the text, all at once, and take what it needs from them. They called the design the transformer. The looking is called attention.

The name is the researchers'. It is not attention in a psychologist's sense of selective focus. It is closer to context: how much each word leans on each of the others.

## What that shows

In the first sentence "it" leaned most on the animal, and in the second on the street. One word at the end changed where the model looked. We picked this example because it is the clearest. The model looks at a sentence in many ways at once, and most of them show nothing this neat.

Do not read too much into the picture. It shows where the model looked. It does not show what the model understood. A brain scan is the same: it shows what lit up, and not what the person was thinking.

## From this to a chat system

The model above can see the words on both sides of "it". The models behind chat systems use the same design with one difference. Each word may draw only on the words before it, because their job is to guess the next word, as in chapter 3.

That raises a puzzle. If a chat system sees only what came before, how can it tell what "it" means when the clue comes later? By the time it reads your message, your whole message is what came before. Only its own answer is written one word at a time.

The design had one more advantage. A computer can take in all the words of a text together, and not one after another. That let researchers give it far more text to practice on and make it far larger. The network in chapter 2 has 25,818 numbers. GPT-3, built on this design in 2020, has 175 billion.

**So what changed in 2017?** A design in which every word can draw on every other word at once. It could be grown far larger. Today's systems are that design, grown large.
