# Inside a chat window

**Is it in the AI?**

This is the first of my mom's three questions. I had used an AI system to build a course for my team, and she wanted to know whether the AI now had the course.

In chapter 3 you saw that a language model does one thing. Given some text, it rates every word that could come next. A chat is built on top of that with one trick.

## What that means

The model is a file of numbers. A file is a saved piece of information on a computer, like a photo or a document. This one is like the 25,818 numbers in chapter 2, only far larger. Using the model does not change the file. Nothing you type is written into the file. When a reply is finished, the model holds nothing of your conversation.

What makes a chat feel like a conversation is the document you just saw. Each time you send a message, the whole conversation so far is sent again, and the model continues it. A new chat starts a new document.

Picture a counselor with no memory at all, who reads the full record of every session, word for word, before each new one. The model is that counselor. The company that runs it is the records office that keeps the file.

## Is it a bunch of computers?

Using a model to get an answer is called inference. Computer chips do it: a chip is the small part of a computer that does arithmetic, and inference is chips doing sums with the numbers in the model's file to rate the next word. That is what it means for a model to run. Building the model, by the practice in chapters 1 to 3, is called training.

Where inference happens depends on the model's size. The network in chapter 2 is small, and it runs on the phone or tablet in your hand. Some phones now carry a small language model of their own. Claude is far too large for that, so it runs on the company's computers.

It is a great many computers, but not one mind. The company says it uses over a million chips of just one type to build and run Claude. Services like this run many copies of the same file, and each copy does the same job on its own.

## So where was my course?

Not inside the model.

- The course was a set of files on my laptop.
- The conversation in which we built it was sent, turn by turn, to the company that runs the model, as a document like the one above.
- The model's numbers did not change at all.

So the model does not have my course. The company did have a record of our conversation, and that is the part worth knowing about.

I used Claude, which is made by a company called Anthropic. A caution first: an AI made by Anthropic wrote this part about Anthropic, and the links at the bottom let you check it. These are its published rules as of November 2026.

A conversation you do not delete is kept, and the rules I read give no fixed limit. When you delete one, it is removed from the company's storage within 30 days, with some exceptions, such as conversations flagged for breaking the rules.

On its personal plans, you choose whether your chats can be used to train its models (to adjust their numbers). Conversations flagged for safety review can be used either way. For its business products, conversations are not used to train its models unless the customer chooses to allow it. Other companies have their own rules, and they are worth reading.

**So, is it in the AI?** Not in the model. The model is a fixed file, and nothing you send is written into it. What does exist is a record of the conversation, kept by the company that runs the model. Whether that record can be used later, when the company builds a new model, depends on its rules and your settings. So the plain answer is: not now, and possibly later, if the rules and settings allow it.
