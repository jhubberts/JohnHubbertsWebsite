# Where the examples come from

**What does "training the AI" mean? Is it like teaching?**

Somebody I know saw a temporary job posting for "AI training". My mom asked me what that job would be, and whether <Term id="training">training</Term> an AI is like teaching a person.

Training means showing a <Term id="model">model</Term> (the trained set of numbers, like the network in chapter 4) <Term id="example">examples</Term> with the right answers, measuring how wrong its answers are, and adjusting its numbers a little to be less wrong, many times over.[^google-glossary] You have seen it already: in chapter 3 the machine moved its line a little each time a flower was on the wrong side, and in chapter 4 the <Term id="neural-network">network</Term>'s 25,818 numbers were nudged after every small group of digits. The right answer attached to an example is called a label.

People write the labels, and some of the labels they write are wrong. Choose how many are wrong here, and watch what the machine learns.

The machine has no way to know which labels are wrong. It learns what it is shown.

## Who wrote the examples

People made each of these collections of examples.

- **The irises in chapter 3.** A botanist, Edgar Anderson, measured them in the summer of 1935. The two kinds grew together in one pasture on the Gaspé Peninsula in Quebec.[^iris-source]
- **The handwritten digits in chapter 4.** Half were written by employees of the US Census Bureau, the government office that counts the population, and half by high-school students.[^mnist]
- **Pictures of everyday things.** A collection called ImageNet holds about 15 million photos sorted into 22,000 kinds of thing. Almost 50,000 people in 167 countries sorted them, paid by the task through an online work site.[^imagenet]

## Practice that needs no labels

<Term id="language-model">Language models</Term>, which chapter 6 explains, guess the next word in a
text. They practice without labels. Their job is to guess the next word, so any text is its own
answer key: hide the next word, guess it, then check. GPT-2, the model in chapter 6, learned this
way from about 8 million web pages.[^gpt2]

Language models practice on far more text than a person ever hears. One model released in 2024 practiced on more than 15 trillion <Term id="word-piece">pieces of words</Term>.[^llama3] Michael Frank, a psychologist at Stanford, estimates that these models get 10,000 to 100,000 times more language than a child hears while learning to talk.[^frank]

## People shaping the answers

Guessing next words alone does not make a model behave like a helpful assistant, so people also shape its answers. In 2022, OpenAI, the company that makes ChatGPT, hired about 40 people to write example answers and to rank the model's answers from best to worst. The model's numbers were then adjusted so that answers like the preferred ones became more likely. People liked a small model trained this way better than a model 100 times its size trained without it.[^instructgpt]

Writing and ranking answers like these is most of what the job postings ask for. By 2024 AI also wrote many of the examples: Meta, the company that owns Facebook, says that other models wrote "the vast majority" of the example answers for one 2024 model.[^llama31]

## The job posting

The postings I found are remote and paid by the hour, usually as contract or part-time work. The tasks are writing hard questions in your own field, writing the correct answers, checking facts, and rating and ranking a model's answers. Pay runs from up to $17 an hour for general rating to over $100 an hour for doctors, lawyers and other experts.[^dataannotation][^handshake]

Some of this work is training AI to do the worker's own job. In 2025 OpenAI paid more than 100 former investment bankers $150 an hour to build financial models, the spreadsheets bankers use to value a company, for a project aimed at the routine work of junior bankers.[^bankers]

Some of the work pays far less. In 2021 and 2022, workers in Kenya earned about $1.32 to $2 an hour reading and labeling violent and abusive text, so that ChatGPT would learn to avoid it. One of them told TIME the work "was torture".[^time-kenya]

## Is it like teaching?

Training is like teaching in four ways: there are examples, there is correction, the learner improves with practice, and it learns mistakes as faithfully as facts. Given wrong labels, it learns from them anyway, as a student would from a wrong textbook.

It differs from teaching in three ways.

- **Nobody can ask it to explain.** A teacher can ask a student why. Training only measures whether the answers match, and changes the model's numbers by arithmetic.
- **It needs vastly more examples.** A child learns to talk from a tiny fraction of the language a model needs.
- **The teacher may be training a replacement.** A student does not usually take over the teacher's job.

**So, what does "training the AI" mean?** Showing a model examples that people wrote, checked or ranked, and adjusting its numbers until its answers match. It is like teaching in the examples and the corrections. It is unlike teaching in that nobody checks what the model understands, and the person doing the training may be helping it do their own job.
