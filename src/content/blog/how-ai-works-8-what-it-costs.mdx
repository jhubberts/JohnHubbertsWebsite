# What it costs

**What does all this cost?**

Machines in a building called a data center work out every answer from a chat system. The machines use electricity, and the building uses water to keep them cool.

The companies have started to publish how much they use. Their figures are small, they do not agree with each other, and as far as I could find, nobody outside the companies has checked them.

<OneQuestionsFootprint />

## Small each time, large in total

By the companies' own figures, one text answer uses about as much electricity as an LED light bulb uses in two minutes.

The total is much larger. Data centers used about 1.5% of the world's electricity in 2024. The International Energy Agency expects about 3% by 2030, and says AI accounts for almost half of the growth. In the United States the share was 4.7% in 2024, and a national laboratory expects nearly 12% by 2030.

## What it learned from

The companies say their models learn from text that is publicly available on the internet, text they pay for, and text their users agree to share. They do not publish a list.

Authors and publishers have gone to court over this. In June 2025 a judge in the United States ruled that training on books a company had bought was allowed, and that downloading pirated books was not. The company was Anthropic, which makes the AI that wrote this series. It agreed to pay $1.5 billion, about $3,000 a book, and a court approved the payment in July 2026.

In September 2026 an appeals court ruled against a different company, which had copied a rival's work to build a competing product. As of late October 2026, a case brought by the New York Times against OpenAI had not been decided, as far as I could find.

## Jobs

It is early, and the studies do not agree.

Researchers at Stanford followed payroll records up to June 2026. They looked at workers aged 22 to 25 in the kinds of job where AI can do the most. About 19% fewer of them were employed than if those jobs had kept pace with other jobs, mostly because fewer were hired. The researchers found no job loss across the economy as a whole. They say their numbers show a pattern and do not prove that AI caused it.

A study in Denmark found no effect on pay or hours in the first two years after ChatGPT appeared.

## Elections

Before the elections of 2024, many people expected AI-made fakes to change results. A review by researchers at Columbia University found that this did not happen.

Experiments since then have found that a chatbot arguing for a candidate can shift the views of people in a survey by a few points, and in two countries by about ten.

In the United States elections of 2026, one university project counted at least 164 political ads made with AI, costing at least $80 million. Most carried no label saying so. I have not yet found a study of what effect they had.

**So what does it cost?** One answer costs very little. All of it together uses a growing share of the world's electricity. The other costs fall on the people whose writing AI learned from, on people starting their working lives, and on elections. Those are still being worked out, in courts and in studies.
